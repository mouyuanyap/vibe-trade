### Competitive Landscape & Moat Assessment

Apple's competitive moat remains among the widest in global technology, anchored by three structural advantages:

1. **Ecosystem lock-in** — 2.2B+ active devices create switching costs that no competitor can replicate. Services revenue hit an all-time record of $31B in Q2 FY2026, demonstrating monetization depth.
2. **iPhone dominance** — Apple captured 48% of global smartphone market revenue in Q1 2026, its highest-ever first-quarter share. iPhone 17 and iPhone 17 Pro Max were the world's two best-selling phones.
3. **Capital return** — The $100B buyback authorization and $28B quarterly operating cash flow provide a valuation floor that peers cannot match.

**Threats to the moat:**
- **China competition**: Huawei and Vivo have overtaken Apple in unit sales within China. While Greater China revenue grew +28% YoY in Q2, this reflects product cycle strength rather than structural share gains. China's tech self-sufficiency push and export controls on rare earths are long-term risks.
- **Supply chain concentration**: Apple is accelerating its India manufacturing shift, targeting all US-sold iPhone production from India by end of 2026. This is positive for tariff risk mitigation but introduces execution risk.
- **AI strategy divergence**: Unlike MSFT/AMZN/GOOGL spending $200B+ on AI data centers, Apple is taking a capital-light approach (partnering with Google for Siri/Gemini). This preserves margins but risks falling behind if on-device AI becomes table stakes. The bull case: Apple owns the device layer and can integrate any winning AI model.

**Regulatory risk**: EU Digital Markets Act compliance costs are ongoing. US tariff policy remains the largest near-term wildcard — Apple's China-based supply chain is directly exposed.
