### Economic Cycle Positioning

The US economy is showing **early stagflationary signals**: Q1 2026 GDP decelerated to 2.0% (below consensus 2.4%) while core PCE surged to 3.2% from 2.7% in Q4 2025. Consumer spending — 70% of GDP — is decelerating. The Fed is trapped on hold at 3.75%, unable to cut due to sticky inflation and unwilling to hike into slowing growth. On the Merrill Lynch clock, this maps to a **mid-to-late cycle** with stagflation risk rising.

**Rate path implication for semis:** A 1pp change in discount rates shifts DCF values ~15-20% for high-beta growth names. With AMD's beta at 2.4-2.6, rate sensitivity is amplified. The current "hold" environment is neutral for semis, but a hawkish surprise (or hot CPI print) would disproportionately hit AMD given its extreme multiple expansion.

### Competitive Landscape & Moat Assessment

AMD's competitive moat is **strengthening but not widening as fast as the stock price implies**:

- **Data Center CPU**: AMD holds ~25-30% unit share vs Intel, with EPYC Turin delivering leadership performance/watt. This moat is solid and widening as Intel struggles with foundry execution.
- **AI GPU**: AMD's MI300X/ MI350 series claims 10-15% of the AI accelerator market, but NVDA holds ~80%+ with CUDA lock-in. AMD's ROCm ecosystem is improving but remains the #1 competitive risk. The MI400 roadmap (2026 H2) is critical.
- **Client PC**: Steady ~20% share, cyclical exposure. Not a moat driver.
- **FPGA (Xilinx)**: Defensible niche in embedded/defense, but integration synergies are still emerging.
- **Key risk**: Hyperscalers (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia) are developing custom AI silicon that could cap AMD's AI GPU addressable market. This is a 3-5 year threat, not immediate.

### Geopolitical & Regulatory Risk

- **Taiwan Strait**: AMD depends on TSMC for leading-edge manufacturing. Any cross-strait escalation is an existential supply risk. Current risk: moderate but contained.
- **China export controls**: AMD's MI300/400 series is restricted from China sales. Revenue exposure to China is primarily via client PC CPUs, not AI accelerators. The impact of further export restrictions would be manageable.
- **Tariff risk**: The US-Iran truce (May 2026) has eased near-term geopolitical tension, but semiconductor-specific tariffs remain a tail risk under any administration.
