### Competitive Landscape & Moat Assessment

Amazon's competitive moat rests on three structural pillars:

1. **AWS infrastructure moat** — AWS commands ~30% market share with a $150B annualized run rate and $364B backlog. The switching costs for enterprise cloud migrations are extraordinarily high, creating a "razor-and-blades" model where initial migration leads to expanding workloads. AI/ML services (SageMaker, Bedrock) are layering on additional stickiness. AWS margins are expanding as infrastructure utilization improves.

2. **E-commerce logistics moat** — Amazon's fulfillment network (same-day delivery, 200M+ Prime members) creates a barrier to entry that no competitor can replicate. The shift to regional fulfillment has reduced cost-to-serve while increasing delivery speed. Third-party seller services (FBA, advertising) layer high-margin revenue on top of the logistics infrastructure.

3. **Advertising flywheel** — Amazon Ads leverages closed-loop purchase data that Google and Meta cannot access. Ad revenue is high-margin (~40%+ incremental margin) and is the fastest-growing segment. Prime Video ads open the upper-funnel opportunity.

**Threats to the moat:**
- **Antitrust**: FTC lawsuit (filed 2023) alleging monopoly power in online marketplace and fulfillment services. Trial is ongoing but unlikely to produce major remedies within the 30-day window.
- **AWS competition**: Azure is gaining share (~25%) with enterprise relationships and OpenAI/Copilot integration. GCP is growing faster (+63% vs AWS +28%) from a smaller base.
- **Retail margin pressure**: Wage inflation, fuel costs, and $200B 2026 capex plan compress retail margins. The consumer is resilient now but vulnerable to a recession.
- **Capex intensity**: $200B planned capex for 2026 (largely AI infrastructure) is an unprecedented investment cycle that creates execution risk and FCF pressure.
