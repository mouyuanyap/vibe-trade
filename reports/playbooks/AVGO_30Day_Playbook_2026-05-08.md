## SECTION 1 — Macro & Industry Context

The US economy is in stagflationary-lite (GDP +2.0%, PCE 4.5%, Fed on hold at 3.50-3.75%). For Broadcom — a diversified semiconductor and infrastructure software company — the macro presents a tale of two businesses: AI semiconductor revenue (+106% YoY to $8.4B) is decoupled from the macro cycle, while legacy semiconductor and enterprise software are more cyclical.

Broadcom operates at the intersection of two mega-trends: **AI infrastructure buildout** (custom ASICs for hyperscalers like GOOGL's TPU, Meta's MTIA) and **enterprise software consolidation** (VMware integration). The AI custom silicon market is projected to grow from ~$20B in 2025 to $50B+ by 2028 as hyperscalers increasingly design their own chips. Broadcom is the dominant player with an estimated 55-60% market share in AI ASICs.

The **semiconductor industry** is in expansion. WSTS forecasts 26%+ semiconductor growth in 2026, led by AI and memory. Networking (Ethernet switches, PCIe, DSPs) — where Broadcom is #1 or #2 in most categories — benefits from AI data center buildout. VMware's enterprise software provides recurring revenue (~$12B+ annualized) that smooths semiconductor cyclicality.

### Competitive Moat Assessment

Broadcom's moat is anchored by:
1. **AI ASIC dominance** — Broadcom designs custom AI accelerators for 3 of the top 4 hyperscalers (GOOGL TPU, Meta MTIA, plus a third unnamed customer). The ASIC design process takes 18-24 months and creates deep lock-in once a customer commits. Margins on custom silicon are 60%+ gross.
2. **Networking monopoly** — Broadcom holds #1 share in Ethernet switches (Tomahawk/Jericho), PCIe switches, and optical DSPs. AI clusters require massive networking bandwidth — Broadcom captures silicon content at every layer.
3. **VMware recurring revenue** — The VMware acquisition transformed Broadcom's business model. VMware contributes ~$12B+ in annual recurring software revenue with ~70%+ gross margins, providing a stable cash flow base.
4. **Hock Tan's M&A playbook** — Broadcom's CEO has a proven track record of acquiring mature tech assets, cutting costs, and extracting cash flows. The VMware integration is on track.

**Threats:**
- **In-house ASIC migration**: Hyperscalers could bring ASIC design fully in-house (GOOGL already designs TPU internally; Broadcom provides physical implementation). AVGO's value-add is in physical design, packaging, and manufacturing — harder to in-source than architecture.
- **NVDA competition**: NVIDIA's GB300 platform integrates networking (NVLink, ConnectX) that competes with Broadcom's Ethernet switching in AI clusters.
- **Apple in-sourcing**: Apple is developing its own modem/WiFi-BT chips, potentially displacing Broadcom components in iPhones over the next 2-3 years. This is a $5-10B annual revenue risk but not in the 30-day window.
