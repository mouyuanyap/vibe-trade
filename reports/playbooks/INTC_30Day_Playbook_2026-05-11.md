for three consecutive months (52.7 in April, highest in 37 months). GDP rebounded to
+2.0% in Q1 2026 after a soft Q4 2025 (0.5%). However, inflation is sticky — CPI rose
to 3.3% in March, and core PCE remains above the Fed's 2% target, pushing rate-cut
expectations out and introducing **hike risk**.

The Fed held rates at 3.50–3.75% at the April 30 FOMC meeting. Multiple Fed officials
(including Boston Fed President Collins) have since signaled that rate **hikes** may
need to be on the table if inflation does not resume its downward path. This is a
material shift from the "cuts later this year" consensus that prevailed in Q4 2025.

**Implication for semis:** The semiconductor sector has been a primary beneficiary of
AI CapEx-driven growth, which is rate-insensitive in the near term. However, further
Fed hawkishness would compress the valuation multiples that have driven the April rally.
A 1pp change in the discount rate shifts DCF value by ~15–20% for growth stocks.

- **Export controls:** US-China tech restrictions continue evolving. Intel has

- **Government stake:** The US government took a ~10% stake in Intel during the

  the day before the dot-com bubble peaked.
- Intel shares peaked at $73.94 in March 2000 and were trading at $35.09 seventeen

- The 2000 cycle ended via: (a) Fed rate hikes (6.5% by May 2000), (b) demand

  builds. However, the valuation expansion velocity is similar.

- Duration: ~18 months from trough to peak. Current rally: ~10 months from Intel's
  July 2025 low (~$20) to current (~$125) — a 6x move in less than a year.

**"This time is different" thesis:** AI inference TAM is a new demand driver that
didn't exist in prior cycles. Hyperscaler CapEx is contracted 3+ years out, and
Intel's foundry pivot (18A, Apple/Samsung talks, Terafab) represents a structural
business model transformation, not just cyclical expansion.

**Counterpoint:** Every cycle peak had a "this time is different" narrative.
