### Historical Cycle Parallels

**Parallel 1: Semiconductor Cycle 1998–2000 (Dot-Com Era)**
- Then: Intel, Cisco, Microsoft drove tech capex; semiconductor index rose ~400% from
  1998 to March 2000 peak; PE multiples reached 60-80x for leaders
- NVDA today: Forward PE 24.2x, TTM PE 40.1x — valuations are elevated by historical
  standards but NOT at dot-com bubble levels on forward basis
- What ended the 2000 cycle: Rate hikes (Fed raised 175bp), Y2K capex digestion, and
  demand exhaustion as dot-coms went bankrupt
- **Key difference:** In 2000, most dot-coms had no earnings. Today's AI capex is funded
  by the most profitable companies on earth (Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon).

**Parallel 2: Post-GFC Tech Recovery 2009–2012**
- Then: Mobile/internet infrastructure buildout drove semis; several years of sustained
  above-trend growth before mean-reversion
- More structurally comparable to today's AI infrastructure buildout
- Duration: ~3 years of above-trend growth, then PE compression from 25x to 15x

**If "this time is different":** The AI inference TAM is a genuinely new demand driver
that didn't exist in prior cycles. Hyperscaler capex is contracted 3+ years out with
firm purchase commitments. Unlike 2000, these are not speculative startups spending
VC money — they're the largest cash-flow-generating companies in history.
