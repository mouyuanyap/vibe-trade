The US is in mid-to-late expansion (Fed funds 3.50–3.75%, core PCE 3.2%, ISM PMI 52.7). Rate cuts are not imminent — sticky inflation and Powell's Chair transition (May 15) add policy uncertainty. This favors value and cash-rich names over pure-growth stories.

### Ride-Hailing & Delivery Industry

The ASEAN ride-hailing market is estimated at **$26.2B in 2026**, growing at 7.3% CAGR toward $37.2B by 2031 (Mordor Intelligence). Post-COVID mobility is fully recovered and growing — Grab's Mobility GMV grew 23% YoY in Q1 2026. Food delivery demand remains robust (Deliveries GMV +25% YoY) with no signs of post-pandemic normalization drag.

**The competitive landscape is rationalizing.** GoTo reported its first-ever quarterly profit in Q1 2026 (IDR 171B net income), signaling an end to the scorched-earth price wars. Grab's acquisition of Delivery Hero's foodpanda Taiwan business ($600M, closing H2 2026) marks its first expansion outside SE Asia and increases its addressable food-delivery market by ~20%.

**Moat assessment**: Grab's network effects (50.5M MTUs, +17% YoY), multi-service cross-sell (6 products per user on average), and deep local integration create a wide regional moat. New entrants (Bolt, inDrive) are expanding but face the same localization barriers. **Moat: WIDE but under-monetized.**

### THE CRITICAL RISK: Indonesia Commission Cap

On **May 1, 2026**, President Prabowo announced a presidential regulation cutting the maximum commission ride-hailing platforms can charge drivers from **20% to 8%**. Citi estimates a $35–40M annualized EBITDA impact if covering 4-wheelers (~5% of mobility EBITDA), or $5–10M if only 2-wheelers. Implementation timing and scope remain uncertain. **This is the single largest near-term overhang on the stock** and has driven post-earnings weakness despite a Q1 beat.
