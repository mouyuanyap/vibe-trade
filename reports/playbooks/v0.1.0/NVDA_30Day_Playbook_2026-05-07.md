## SECTION 1 — Macro & Industry Landscape

### Economic Cycle Position

The US economy is in **mid-to-late expansion**. Q1 2026 GDP grew 2.0% annualized. ISM Manufacturing PMI at 52.7 marks the 4th consecutive month of expansion after a 10-month contraction. But sticky inflation (core PCE 3.2%, headline CPI 3.3%) has the Fed holding at 3.50–3.75% with no cut until at least H2 2026. Four FOMC dissents at the April 29 meeting signal internal division. Powell's Chair term expires **May 15** — a leadership transition adds policy uncertainty.

**Implication for NVDA**: Semiconductors are long-duration assets. A 1pp change in discount rate shifts DCF value by ~15–20%. With WACC at ~16%, NVDA's valuation is pinned to earnings delivery, not multiple expansion. The rate backdrop is a headwind, not a tailwind.

### Semiconductor Industry

The SOX posted its **strongest month in 32 years** in April 2026 (17 consecutive up days, +$5.45B combined SMH+SOXX inflows). WSTS forecasts 26%+ global semi market growth in 2026 toward ~$975B. Bank of America frames this as the **midpoint** of an 8–10 year structural AI infrastructure cycle, not a peak. Supply remains tight — TSMC, Micron, Broadcom all report growing backlogs and high utilization. No signs of oversupply in leading-edge AI silicon.

**Implication for NVDA**: The structural demand thesis is intact. Record ETF inflows signal strong institutional conviction. The broadening rally (AMD +80% vs NVDA +10.5% over the past 6 months) is a rotational signal but not a negative one — money is flowing into the sector, not out.

### AI Capex: The Demand Engine

Hyperscaler capex (MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META) is projected at **$725B in 2026**, up 77% YoY from ~$410B in 2025. Individual guides: Google $180–190B, Microsoft ~$120B+, Amazon ~$100B. However, all three stocks sold off post-earnings as investors scrutinize ROI on this spending. This creates a tension: the capex is NVDA's revenue, but ROI skepticism could eventually slow orders.

**Implication for NVDA**: Demand visibility is excellent for the next 2–3 quarters. The risk is medium-term (2027+), not this 30-day window. The upcoming Rubin platform (H2 2026) and Computex (June 1) are demand catalysts.

### Competitive Moat Assessment

NVDA holds ~80% AI GPU share. CUDA + full-stack integration + annual product cadence (Blackwell → Rubin → Rubin Ultra → Feynman) remain a wide moat. But the moat is being **incrementally narrowed** at the edges:

- **Google TPU v6**: Deployed at scale, aggressive inference pricing ($0.39/chip-hour)
- **Amazon Trainium 3**: In development; CEO reaffirmed NVDA partnership but custom silicon is a long-term TAM threat
- **Microsoft Maia**: Hundreds of thousands of chips expected in production by end-2026
- **AMD MI400**: Launching H2 2026; MI350P PCIe launched today (May 7)
- **China**: Huawei Ascend 910B/C but SMIC yields are poor; the gap with NVDA remains wide

**Assessment**: NVDA's moat is wide for the next 12–18 months. The competitive threat is real but more relevant to 2027–2028 thesis than this 30-day window.

### Institutional Flow Signal

April 2026 was a record month: **SMH +$3.4B, SOXX +$2.05B** combined inflows — the highest monthly figure on record. QQQ (NVDA ~8% weight) remains the primary vehicle for retail/institutional tech exposure. Flow direction is unequivocally **accumulation**.
